# AEGISSTOR PHOENIX — Known Limitations & Research Boundaries

## 1. Simulation Fidelity vs. Physical Hardware Dynamics
- **Mode A Simulation Assumption**: The Digital Twin scenario engine models capacity reclaim and latency stabilization based on theoretical queueing equations and deterministic empirical estimates. It does not model physical hardware quirks (such as flash controller thermal throttling, silent bit rot, or SAS expander lane errors).
- **Synthetic Telemetry Boundaries**: In local evaluation mode, telemetry traces are generated by `SyntheticGenerator`. While they incorporate diurnal seasonality and stochastic noise, they cannot fully replicate corner cases encountered in million-IOPS hyperscale environments.

## 2. Distributed Consensus & Multi-Node Orchestration
- **Single Orchestrator Instance**: Current SQLite / local database architecture is designed for single-node evaluation and lab experimentation. Multi-region high availability with Raft-based consensus across redundant orchestrators is not yet implemented.
- **Synchronous Policy Gate**: Policy evaluation occurs synchronously on proposal submission. In fleets with hundreds of thousands of volumes, distributed asynchronous policy evaluation caches will be required.

## 3. Autonomous Physical Mutation Policy
- By design, `MODE_C_CONTROLLED_EXECUTION` is hard-locked. Physical volume expansion, driver-level TRIM dispatch, or physical disk evacuation requires manual operator integration with storage vendor REST APIs (such as Pure Storage FlashArray REST, NetApp ONTAP API, or Ceph Orchestrator).
